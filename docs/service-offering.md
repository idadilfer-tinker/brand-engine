# Service Offering

## Who this is for

Venture-backed, AI-led startups and scale-ups -- seed through Series C -- who are shipping fast, producing marketing/product surfaces across many channels simultaneously, and don't have (or can't yet afford) a full in-house brand team to keep it all consistent. Ideal signs a prospect is a fit: multiple people (founders, growth, product) are already generating their own assets with AI tools, with visibly inconsistent results; the existing brand kit is a static PDF or Figma file nobody outside design opens; they're scaling content/campaign velocity faster than their design headcount; they compete in a category (AI tooling, fintech, dev tools) where every competitor looks the same, and differentiation has to come from execution, not the pitch.

## Engagement tiers

Tier 1 -- Engine Audit. A short, fixed-scope diagnostic. We evaluate existing brand assets (if any) for reusable equity, identify candidate primitives, and map where the current system will break under distributed/AI-assisted production. Deliverable: a findings report and recommendation on whether the client needs a refresh (Tier 2) or a from-zero build (Tier 3). Best for: startups unsure whether their current identity is salvageable.

Tier 2 -- Engine Refresh. For startups with existing visual equity worth evolving (the Stripe model). We take 1-3 existing motifs, give them a unified material language (color, depth, motion), and build the full three-layer engine: primitives, documented rule set, operable skill. Best for: startups past their first identity, entering a scale-up phase, whose current brand is recognizable but inconsistent.

Tier 3 -- Engine Build. From-zero identity built engine-first from day one: primitives, shared DNA, and the skill layer designed together rather than retrofitted. Best for: newly funded startups defining their identity for the first time, especially pre-Series A teams who want to skip the "static kit" phase entirely.

Tier 4 -- Engine Retainer. Ongoing stewardship after handoff: quarterly system audits as the product/company evolves, new primitive additions as new product lines launch, skill updates as AI tooling changes. Optional, month-to-month. Best for: scale-ups where the brand needs to keep evolving alongside a fast product roadmap.

## Process

Step 1, Discovery -- existing equity audit, category/competitor scan, format inventory (every surface the brand currently needs to work across, plus ones it'll plausibly need in 12 months).

Step 2, Primitive selection -- narrow to 2-4 primitives; validate each against at least one format outside the current brief.

Step 3, DNA definition -- establish the shared token layer (color, material/surface, motion, geometry) that lets primitives combine or stand alone.

Step 4, Rule authoring -- write the combination logic precisely enough to be machine-checkable, not just human-readable.

Step 5, Asset production -- build hero assets across priority formats to prove the system, mirroring how Estudi-Image proved the Stripe system (billboard, card, phone UI, OOH, stickers).

Step 6, Skill encoding -- package the rule set as an operable skill/agent config for the client's own tools.

Step 7, Handoff and training -- walk the client team through generating a net-new asset live, using the skill, before the engagement closes.

Step 8 (optional), Retainer -- move to Tier 4 for ongoing stewardship.

## What makes this different from a standard branding agency engagement

Standard agencies hand over a static kit and a PDF of guidelines -- the same model Estudi-Image used for Stripe, minus the in-house Brand Studio relationship that keeps evolving it. Design-system-focused competitors (Ramotion, Heartbeat, MetaLab, The Branx) have moved a layer further, shipping tokens and components alongside brand identity. The Brand Engine adds the layer none of them are shipping as standard: the rule set encoded as something the client's own AI tools can execute directly, so the system keeps producing correct, on-brand work with zero agency involvement after handoff. See docs/research.md for the market comparison this is based on.

## Pricing logic (structure, not fixed numbers)

Price by primitive complexity and format count, not by hours. A 2-primitive engine covering 5 formats is a materially different scope than a 4-primitive engine covering 15 formats -- price should track scope, and retainer pricing should track how often the client expects to add primitives or formats, not time spent.
